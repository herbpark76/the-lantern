## The Shower

The shower was the size of a small office and had more settings than my first car. I stood under it for a long time with my right hand flat against my chest, a little to the left of the middle, where I had been looking at the drone from.

There was nothing there. I pressed. I moved my hand an inch and pressed again. I am not sure what I was expecting to find, a hole or a scar or a soreness, some small receipt from Grand Avenue. The skin was ordinary. Underneath it my heart was going at what I would estimate was its usual rate, which I had never in my life thought to check.

I found that I knew where the towels were. I found that I knew which of the suits in the closet was for days when something was on the wire, a dark blue one with a lining the color of a bruise, and I put it on, and it fit me perfectly, which is more than I can say for most of the suits I have paid to have fitted.

## Stress

While I was doing up the cuffs, I came up with a theory.

I want to set it down fairly, because it was a good theory, and for about ten minutes it held up very well. The theory was that I was under a great deal of stress. Whoever I was, I was a man who ran a company that put a number on two hundred million people, and something was apparently on the wire in Indonesia, and I had not had a holiday since a skiing trip in 2014 on which I took three conference calls from a chairlift. Men like that are known to break. I had read about it, in the kind of magazine that is left on the seats of private planes. A bond trader in Connecticut had driven to work one morning and forgotten, completely and permanently, the previous eleven years. A shipping heir woke up in a hotel in Lisbon convinced he was his own brother. It had a name. There were clinics for it, discreet ones, with gardens. Most of the men recovered. One of them wrote a book.

So I had dreamed it. That was the theory. I had dreamed a whole life in Los Angeles, in a glass house, with a different company and a colder marriage, and I had dreamed it so thoroughly and in such detail, the saucer and the peacock and the sunflower seeds, that I had woken up believing it, which was precisely the kind of thing a mind under that kind of strain would do. The dream had even had the decency to end with my death, so that I would know it was over.

I looked at myself in the mirror, in the bruise-colored lining, and I thought: Frank, you are going a little crazy, and it is perfectly understandable, and you will see someone discreet with a garden, and in six months this will be an anecdote.

It was such a relief that I had to sit down on the edge of the bath.

Then I put my hand into the breast pocket of the blue suit, out of habit, the way you check for your keys, and there was a piece of paper in it, folded, thermal, slightly curled. I did not take it out. I knew what it would say. Sixty dollars, pump four, 8:31 a.m.

I sat on the edge of the bath for a while longer. The theory did not go away, exactly. It is still there, if I want it. It simply had to make room.

## Breakfast

Gabriella was in the kitchen in a man's shirt, mine, eating yogurt out of the pot standing up and reading something on her phone. There was music playing from a small speaker on the windowsill, a piano, not very loud. In Bel Air we had a sound system that cost more than the speaker's entire country and was never on.

“You look nice,” she said. “Jakarta suit.”

I sat down at the table. There was bread, and butter in a dish, and a jar of apricot jam with a handwritten label, and I did not know who had made it. I wanted very badly to know who had made it.

“Gabriella,” I said. “Can I tell you something strange?”

She put the yogurt down. In Bel Air, if I had begun a sentence that way, she would have assumed it was about money.

“I died this morning.”

She laughed. It was a real laugh, surprised out of her, the one from the concert program, *I promise to laugh at you when you need it*, and then she looked at my face and stopped laughing, quite slowly, the way you put down something you have just realized is hot.

“Okay,” she said. She pulled out the chair across from me and sat in it. “Tell me.”

So I told her. Not all of it. I told her about the glass house, and the coffee that was wrong, and the helicopters, and the woman's voice telling me to walk backward toward it. I did not tell her about the sniper. I said, “And then something happened, and I woke up here.” I did not tell her about the guest room in Bel Air, or the door I closed, or that in the other life she had stopped playing. I found that I could not say that one out loud, in her kitchen, with her calluses.

She listened to all of it without interrupting, which I would not have predicted. When I finished she was quiet for a while.

“You've been working too hard,” she said. “I mean that. I'm not just saying it.”

“I know.”

“You talked in your sleep. Most of the night.” She turned her coffee cup around on the table, a quarter turn, and then back. “Was I there? In the dream?”

“Yes.”

“Was I happy?”

I thought about lying to her. I had a great deal of practice and some real talent.

“No,” I said. “I don't think so. I don't think I noticed.”

She nodded slowly, as if that were the answer she had expected, and also as if it hurt a little, which is not a combination I had ever seen on her face before. Then she reached over and took a piece of bread off my plate, which she had never once done in eleven years in the other kitchen, and buttered it, and ate it.

“Come tonight,” she said. “Please. Even if Jakarta's on fire. Sit somewhere I can see you.”

“I'll be there.”

“You said that this morning.”

“I meant it this morning,” I said. “I mean it more now.”

She looked at me for a moment longer, the way she looked at a passage of music that she was not sure of yet. Then she got up and kissed the top of my head and went to get dressed, and I sat in the kitchen with the piano and the jam and the receipt in my pocket, and for a few minutes, I would like it noted, I was happy, and I knew that I was, which as far as I can tell had never happened to me before at the same time.
