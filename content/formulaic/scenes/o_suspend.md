## The Board

“Suspend it,” I said. “Every score in Jakarta that moved last night. Put them back where they were until we understand what happened.”

Nobody moved. Sunny looked at me first, then at Hal, then back at me, with the expression of someone who has been arguing for a thing for months and has just been handed it and does not trust the wrapping.

“Frank,” Hal said. “You built the thing so that nobody could do that.”

“Then I'll unbuild it.”

“You're not yourself.”

I thought about that for longer than he expected. “No,” I said. “Probably not.”

The chair of the board called eleven minutes later, from a car somewhere in Connecticut, where it was not yet four in the morning. She had been woken up, she said, by her own phone, which she kept under her pillow, and which had told her what I had done before her chief of staff could. She was very calm. She said the word *precedent* six times. She asked whether I had considered what a score was worth if the man who owned it could take it back whenever he felt sorry for someone, and I said yes, I had, just now, for the first time, and she was quiet for a moment and then she said she would see me at the board meeting on Thursday.

The number on my lock screen went from 909 to 877 while I was still holding the phone.

## Thank You

There was a television in the lobby on my way out, tuned to a business channel with the sound off. It was showing Jalan Thamrin. The crowd was still there, but it had changed shape, the way a crowd does when it is no longer angry and has not yet decided to go home. Near the front someone was holding a sheet of cardboard with two words on it in English, in black marker, a little crooked.

THANK YOU.

I found that I could not look at it for very long. I went out into the street.
