## The Card

“Can I think about it?” I said.

It was a thing I had said to perhaps five thousand people across perhaps five thousand tables, and every one of them had understood it to mean no. Mia did not seem to hear it that way. She nodded, and dug in her bag, and came up with a business card and a pen.

The card was plain and white. *Dr. M. Ritchie*, it said, and the name of the clinic, and a telephone number with too many digits, the way Swiss numbers have. She turned it over to write her own number on the back, and stopped.

“Oh,” she said.

There was already something written there, in blue ink, in a hand that was not hers. She turned it so I could see.

“Did you write that?” I said.

“No.” She frowned at it. “They come in a box of five hundred. I've never seen that before.” She wrote her number underneath, small, and pushed it across the tablecloth. “Call me. Or don't. He asks for you every morning at the same time. Seven forty.”

She stood up and picked up her bag and went out, and the waiter came back and set down the veal as if nothing had happened, which as far as he was concerned was true.

I ate it. It was very good. It had been good for eighty years. My number, when I looked, was 880.
