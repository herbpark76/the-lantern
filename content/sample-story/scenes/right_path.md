## The Climb

The hill was steeper than it looked. The traveler's breath came hard, and their legs burned, and the pale shape above did not seem to get any closer.

When they finally reached it, they found it was a chapel, or what had been one. The door hung open. Inside, the pews were gone, and the floor was covered in candle stubs — hundreds of them, some burned to nothing, some still tall.

A woman sat in the corner, mending a coat by the light of a single candle. She looked up when the traveler entered, and she smiled, and the smile was kind and tired and very old.

"You've come," she said, as though she had been expecting them. "Sit. Rest. The road will be there in the morning."

The traveler sat. The woman poured tea from a pot that had no visible source of heat. The tea was warm, and it tasted of honey and something darker.
