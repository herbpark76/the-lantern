## Among the Oaks

The canopy closed overhead like a mouth. The traveler walked by the lantern's light, which here seemed thinner, as though the trees drank it.

They heard water somewhere ahead — a stream, or something wider. The path forked again, but both forks curved toward the sound, and the traveler let their feet choose.

A figure stood beside the path, leaning against an oak. It was made of shadow and bark, and it did not move. It said nothing. But the traveler felt, with a certainty that came from somewhere outside themselves, that it was waiting — and that it had been waiting a long time.

The traveler kept walking. The figure did not follow. But the feeling of being watched did not leave them, not for the rest of the night, and not for the rest of the story.
