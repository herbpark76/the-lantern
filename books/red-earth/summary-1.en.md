# Session 1 Summary

In 57 minutes and 32 answers, she gave us the novel's spine: a girl in 1960s Daegu who could not stand unfairness, torn between two women — a cold, refined mother and a warm, foul-mouthed, generous aunt. She calls the giving side of herself her aunt's gift. Her family called her Jung-won, and her aunt called her "Won-a."

### People

- **Her mother.** Graduated from Kyungbuk Girls' High, then called Daegu Girls' High, under Japanese rule. Refined and exacting about food, clothes and the house; ordered yugwa and yakgwa from Gyeongju for feasts. Never cursed, never said "I'm sorry." Favored her son, gave only where it would pay off, and looked down on the women who worked in the house. Mom stopped respecting her "as a person," and did not go to her when she died.
- **Her father.** Educated, "about the same level" as her mother. Nothing more yet.
- **Her brother.** The son her mother favored. Name unknown.
- **Her older sister.** Explained the farmhands' dirty joke at Uiseong to her.
- **Her aunt and uncle.** Her mother's younger sister: plain-looking, full of warmth, "would be a sophisticated comedian today." A big hanok in Daegu and a business, maybe a textile factory. Feasts for the whole neighborhood, generous New Year's money, curses that made people laugh. She helped hungry people.
- **Her uncle's wife.** Respected, like her aunt.
- **Her grandmother in Uiseong.** A country house with about twelve farmhands, pigs and a cow.
- **Lee Sun-bun.** A poor classmate when Mom was about ten. She lived by a ditch, "like beside a sewer." Her mother sold greens at the market, sitting outside even in winter.
- **The doctor's daughter.** A good-natured fourth-grade classmate whose mother brought gifts to the teacher. Later a close friend at university.
- **Her closest friend.** Met at Kyungpook National University Middle School at 14 or 15. Polio in one leg, no father, played piano. Inseparable for decades; the friendship broke about ten years ago. She wants to tell that story "next time."
- **The household worker.** The woman Mom always sided with when the grown-ups boasted.
- **Her son and daughter.** Her son runs a company. Her daughter is a professor at the University of Sydney, works in film, and loves her stories.
- **Her animals.** Junyoung, a beagle, and Seyoung, a husky, died last year; her grief led to a month in the hospital. Sophie, a mix of both, "brought me back." Two cats, Mia and Jeong-a.

### Timeline so far

- **1951:** born in Daegu.
- **About 1961:** fourth grade. Drops from first to second in music under a new teacher and thinks, "What a filthy world." Buys fried bread for Sun-bun.
- **About 1965:** enters Kyungpook National University Middle School by early admission, about 120 students, and meets her pianist friend.
- **School holidays:** her grandmother's house in Uiseong, lighting straw in the fire hole.
- **1970s:** strict parents; she marries instead of pursuing a career, while friends go to Seoul National University and become painters, musicians and professors.
- **About forty years in America,** and a divorce; when is unknown.
- **Now:** Dallas, age 75, recovering after a hospital stay, with Sophie at her side.

### Threads for the novel

- **Unfairness** is her lifelong compass: music grades bought with gifts, sons over daughters, workers looked down on.
- **Two models of womanhood:** her mother, educated, cold and calculating, and her aunt, plain, earthy and generous. Her whole life is a choice between them.
- **Giving:** a ten-year-old's note, "One for your mom and one for you," still describes her at 75.
- **The road not taken:** classmates who became artists and professors, and "a nameless woman."
- **Sensory anchors:** fried white bread from the army base with watery jam, chrysanthemum-cake carts, jeon frying, beef-bone soup simmering, straw crackling in the fire hole.
